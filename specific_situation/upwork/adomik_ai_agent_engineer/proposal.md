Answering your first question before anything else, because it decides the rest: no. I am in
Port-au-Prince, Haiti, on UTC-5, which sits outside the UTC-1 to UTC+3 range you set.

You were explicit that applications from outside that range will not be reviewed. If the rule is
absolute, discard this one now — no reply needed and none expected. I am sending it because the
underlying requirement, the one the rule exists to protect, is the only part I can actually
guarantee, and it takes a paragraph to show you why.

Haiti moves its clocks with the United States, so the gap to Paris is six hours in both winter and
summer — it never drifts. Your 09:30 to 18:30 is my 03:30 to 12:30, the same two numbers all year.
That is an early start and I am not going to pretend otherwise, but it is a fixed one, and a fixed
offset is the thing a distributed team actually needs. If your constraint is contractual or legal
rather than practical, none of that helps and I understand.

The seven items:

1. Within two hours of Paris: no. UTC-5, six hours behind you.

2. City, country, time zone: Port-au-Prince, Haiti. UTC-5 in winter and UTC-4 in summer, following
US daylight saving, which is what keeps the offset to Paris constant at six hours.

3. Earliest start date: within one week.

4. Weekly availability: 40 hours, Monday to Friday, full-time, 09:30 to 18:30 Paris with the hour
for lunch. Fibre with backup power and a second mobile connection, which where I live is not a
formality.

5. Independent contractor: yes. I have worked as one for years, including long engagements through
this platform.

6. Expected hourly rate: $20, the top of your posted range. If the range is firm at a lower number
for an intermediate engagement, say so rather than screen me out on it.

7. Relevant experience. Five years of professional Python on the backend: REST services in FastAPI
with JWT authentication, role-based access control, the repository pattern and full OpenAPI
documentation. ETL in Apache Beam moving MongoDB collections into BigQuery, written to be idempotent
so reruns did not duplicate rows. Advanced SQL against BigQuery, PostgreSQL, MySQL and SQL Server —
window functions, query optimisation, execution plans — and the habit of reading a plan before
arguing about a slow query. Asynchronous Python and the SQLAlchemy 2.0 async stack.

Before that, and underneath all of it, eight years as an economist at Haiti's Ministry of Planning:
execution monitoring of the national investment plan, and a forecasting and simulation model built
in the statistics unit. That work was explaining variance for a living — why the executed figure
diverged from the programmed one, project by project — which is the same shape as your revenue
variation and anomaly work, on a different ledger. Later, running data quality on a multi-site
programme reporting to USAID, I spent years on the discipline of tracing a disagreeing number back
to the source record that produced it.

Which brings me to the line in your posting I would most want to work on: pipelines where the
numerical computation is deterministic and the language model handles language. I think that is
correct and I think most teams get it wrong in the same direction, letting the model near the
arithmetic because it sounds fluent about it. An agent that explains a revenue movement should be
reading a number that a query computed, not producing one. Claude and Claude Code are my daily
tools, so I know both what they are good for and how confidently they are wrong.

On Ruby, which you list as required, let me be precise rather than vague in either direction. I
learned Ruby and Rails properly — Flatiron School's software engineering program in 2019, which is
built on them — and it is a foundation I still have. What I have not done is ship Rails in
production: my paid backend work has been Python and PHP. So if "professional experience with Ruby"
means production years, I do not have them and will not claim them. If it means someone who can read
your Rails codebase, follow its conventions and be useful in it without spending the first month
learning the language, that I can do.

Two smaller gaps, stated rather than left for you to find. I have not used Snowflake or Bedrock; the
warehouse and cloud work is BigQuery and AWS. MCP I have not built in production, which your posting
treats as learnable, and on that one I would back myself.

French is my native language, English is fluent and is the language I work in daily.
