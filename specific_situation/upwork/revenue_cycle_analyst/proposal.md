Your advert says you want someone who can determine why something happened rather than follow a

economic planning, statistics and econometrics. I should be straightforward with you: my coursework

would rather you know now than after an interview.

2. Current or most recent professional role. Software Engineer (Fullstack) at Tekkod LLC, a US
company, February 2026 to present, fully remote. My most recent dedicated analytical role was Data
Analyst and M&E Consultant at Anseye Pou Ayiti, January to March 2026, where I owned the KPI
definitions for multi-programme reporting.

3. Professional qualifications or examinations completed. None of the ones you list. No CFA, no CPA
or equivalent, no actuarial examinations, no FRM. My quantitative credentials are academic and
practical rather than certified.

4. Financial analysis and financial modelling. Eight years as an economist at Haiti's Ministry of
Planning, in the Economic and Social Planning Directorate. I contributed to the execution monitoring
reports of the national Three-Year Investment Plan: what was programmed against what was actually
executed, project by project, across every sector and department. That is variance analysis on a
public investment portfolio under a different name. In the statistics and modelling unit I
contributed to building the Development Planning Model, a forecasting, simulation and policy impact
analysis tool, and I took part in macroeconomic framing meetings with the Ministry of Economy and
Finance.

5. Excel. Daily, as a reconciliation and modelling surface rather than a place to store data. Pivot
tables and pivot-driven reconciliation workbooks, XLOOKUP and INDEX-MATCH, SUMIFS and COUNTIFS for
conditional aggregation, data validation, Power Query for imports that have to be repeatable, and
scenario tables. I am happy to be tested on this.

6. SQL, Python, Power BI, Tableau. Advanced SQL against PostgreSQL, MySQL, BigQuery and SQL Server -
joins, window functions, query optimisation, indexing. Python with Pandas for recurring analysis,
and I have built production ETL pipelines in Apache Beam moving MongoDB data into BigQuery. Power BI
at expert level, plus Looker Studio and Quarto. Tableau I am learning rather than claiming: my
portfolio work is on Tableau Public and my production BI is Power BI.

7. The most analytically difficult problem I have handled. At Caris Foundation International I ran
data quality on a multi-site health programme reporting to USAID. The same indicator arrived from
three places - collection forms filled in at the sites, a national health information system, and an
integrated MySQL database - and the three did not agree. Arguing about the totals got nowhere. What
worked was tracing each reported figure back to the individual source record it was supposed to come
from and finding where it stopped matching. Two causes accounted for most of it. Duplicate
enrolments across sites inflated a count while every site's own numbers looked correct in isolation.
And a denominator defined slightly differently in two systems produced two rates that were each
internally right and mutually contradictory. Neither is visible in a summary; both are obvious once
you go back to the records. I fixed the definitions at source, built the deduplication into the
reporting, and automated the whole cycle in Python and SQL, which cut manual production time by 40
percent and removed a standing source of transcription error. Investigating why a claim was denied
or underpaid, and reconciling claims against payments, contractual adjustments and patient balances,
looks to me like the same discipline applied to a different ledger.

I work from Port-au-Prince on UTC-5, which is US Eastern, so I overlap your entire working day. I
have attached a CV and a cover letter. I would be glad to do a paid sample task on real data if that
is a more useful test than an interview.

Alexandro Disla
