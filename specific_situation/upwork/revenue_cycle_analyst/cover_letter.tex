I am applying for the \textbf{Financial \& Revenue Cycle Analyst} position. I am an applied economist by training and a data analyst by trade, and I have never worked in medical billing. I am writing anyway because of one sentence in your advert: you want someone who can \textit{determine why something happened rather than simply follow a checklist}. That is a fair description of what I have actually been paid to do for the last decade, in three different domains, none of which I knew when I started.

The closest match is the reconciliation work I did at Caris Foundation International, on a multi-site health programme reporting to USAID. Figures arrived from collection forms filled in at the sites, from a national health information system, and from an integrated MySQL database, and they did not agree. I established the data quality protocols that resolved those disagreements, and the method was always the same: do not argue about the totals, trace each reported figure back to the individual source record it was supposed to come from, and find out where it stopped matching. Duplicate enrolments across sites inflate a count while every site's own numbers look correct. A denominator defined slightly differently in two systems produces two rates that are each internally right and mutually contradictory. Neither is visible in a summary; both are obvious once you go back to the records. Investigating why a claim was denied or underpaid, and reconciling claims against payments, contractual adjustments and patient balances, is that same discipline applied to a different ledger --- and I would rather learn the ledger than pretend I already know it.

On the financial and modelling side, I spent eight years at Haiti's Ministry of Planning. I contributed to the execution monitoring reports of the national Three-Year Investment Plan --- what was programmed against what was actually executed, project by project, across every sector --- which is variance analysis on a public investment portfolio under a different name. In the statistics and modelling unit I contributed to the ``Development Planning Model'', a forecasting, simulation and policy impact tool. Revenue and cash-flow forecasting, break-even work and scenario and sensitivity analysis are the applications I would most want to grow into here, and they are the applications my training was aimed at.

The tooling is not a constraint. I work in Excel daily --- pivot-driven reconciliation workbooks, XLOOKUP and INDEX-MATCH, conditional aggregation, Power Query for repeatable imports, scenario tables. I write advanced SQL against PostgreSQL, MySQL, BigQuery and SQL Server, automate recurring analysis in Python with Pandas, and build reporting in Power BI, where I would place myself at expert level. At Caris that combination cut the manual production time of a recurring report by 40 \% and removed a standing source of transcription error; the reporting dashboards a practice needs to see collection rates and ageing accounts receivable by payer are ordinary work for me.

Two things I would rather you hear from me than discover later. I hold no CFA, CPA or actuarial qualification and have passed none of those examinations; my quantitative credentials are academic and practical rather than certified. And my applied economics coursework at C.T.P.E.A is complete, but the Diplôme d'Études Supérieures is conferred only after the defence of the exit thesis, which I have not yet defended --- so I do not claim an awarded degree, and the official attestation is available on request. If either is disqualifying, I would rather know early. If not, I would welcome the chance to talk about the work.
